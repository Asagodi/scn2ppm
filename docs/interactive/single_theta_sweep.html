How the spike bounds move when you tilt the cone

Four decoders Di sit on a cone about the −y axis at polar angle θ, evenly spaced in azimuth. The four spike bounds Bi (translucent planes) all pass through the common apex on the y-axis by construction. Drag θ to open or close the cone; the six pairwise cosines collapse to two by symmetry — cos Ψadj = cos²θ and cos Ψopp = cos 2θ — and λmax is a function of θ alone.

Story: under 4-fold azimuthal symmetry the Lyapunov exponent is strictly negative for every θ in [12°, 87°], so this whole 1-parameter family stays inside the stable cone. The paper's chaos at K=4 lives in broken-symmetry decoder configurations — the negative pairwise cosine alone is not sufficient.

Method. Symmetric-tilt sampler on the apex-first submanifold: Di = (sin θ cos φi, −cos θ, sin θ sin φi) with φi = 0°, 90°, 180°, 270°. Apex-first drive: αi = cos θ for all i, so all four bounds meet at (0, −1, 0). λmax from Lyapunov QR (800 transient + 1500 record). A truly single-Ψij continuous sweep is geometrically impossible at K=4 (moving one Di generically changes 3 pairwise cosines at once); the honest one-parameter version uses a symmetry that reduces the 6 cosines to 2.
